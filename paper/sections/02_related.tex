\section{Related work}
\paragraph{In-context learning against optimal estimators.} Transformers trained from scratch learn simple function classes
in context and approach least squares on linear regression \citep{garg2022}; on linear models they match gradient descent,
ridge and least squares and approach Bayes estimators as width and depth grow \citep{akyurek2023}; and in-context learning
has been explained as implicit Bayesian inference over latent concepts \citep{xie2022}. Prior-fitted networks approximate the
posterior predictive of their training prior \citep{muller2022}.

\paragraph{Memorisation and generalisation.} The transition from finite-pool to continuous-prior Bayes as pretraining diversity
grows is established for linear regression \citep{raventos2023,panwar2024}, with asymptotic theory \citep{lu2025}, a
rational-strategy account \citep{wurgaft2025}, a learning-kinetics account \citep{nguyen2025}, and a view of task diversity as
coverage of task space \citep{goddard2025}; for sequential data it has been studied with finite sets of Markov chains
\citep{gibson2026,park2025}. Our references play the same roles for continuous-state dynamical systems, with an added
context-only Bayes reference distinct from the population one.

\paragraph{Bayes gaps and information.} Recent theory defines a Bayes gap for in-context learners and shows that the
uncertainty from the task mixture vanishes quickly in the number of examples \citep{wakayama2026}, and decomposes in-context
error information-theoretically \citep{jeon2024}. For Bayes mixtures over i.i.d.\ data, the cumulative relative-entropy risk
of the Bayes strategy equals the mutual information between parameter and observations \citep{haussler1997}; our budget is the
two-prior analogue for one Bayes mixture scored against another, which follows from the chain rule of relative entropy
\citep{cover2006}. In regression with exponentially distributed coefficients, a transformer outperformed least-squares,
ridge and $\ell_1$-based estimators for small to moderate context lengths, which the authors read as implicit prior
inference \citep{cao2026}. Point-prediction comparisons are fragile for telling posteriors apart \citep{aswadi2026}; we score
full predictive distributions with the log score \citep{gneiting2007}; differences in expected log score between two density
forecasts are the quantity that likelihood-ratio tests of predictive ability compare \citep{amisano2007}. Whether large language models are Bayesian in context
has been tested through the martingale property \citep{falck2024}, a test built for exchangeable data, which autoregressive
data are not.

\paragraph{Dynamical systems.} Generalisation bounds for in-context learning cover prompts generated by dynamical systems
\citep{li2023algorithms}; transformers trained on linear autoregressive sequences can implement a gradient step on the
transition matrix \citep{sander2024}; a transformer can represent the Kalman filter \citep{goel2024kalman}, and transformers
trained on many systems approach it on unseen ones \citep{du2023filtering,akram2026}; in-context learning of noisy linear
dynamical systems has approximation theory with least-squares references \citep{cole2025}. These works compare networks with
the Kalman filter or least squares, not with population, library and context-only Bayes. Context-only strategies can rival
foundation models on dynamical data \citep{zhang2026parroting}. In a toy example in the main text, an amortised forecaster
trained on a Bayesian AR(1) prior outperforms the AR(1) model in squared error and log predictive score ``since the
Bayesian model knows a little more information'' \citep{koshelev2023}; that is the population-advantage idea for one prior,
without a finite-library or a separate context-only Bayes reference, a separability analysis, or pre-registration.

\paragraph{Design and pre-registration.} Asking where rival models' predictions differ before collecting data is the subject
of optimal design for model discrimination \citep{boxhill1967,atkinson1975}, extended to non-normal models
\citep{lopezfidalgo2007}; our maps are design-space plots of the expected log-score difference between rival predictives,
used here to decide where a behavioural study can classify a model. Seed variance and stratified inference over runs
\citep{agarwal2021,sellam2022} shape our hierarchical bootstrap; pre-registration in machine learning has precedents
\citep{bertinetto2021}. We did not find, in the sources we searched, a map of the separability of Bayes references for
in-context learners made before any network exists.
