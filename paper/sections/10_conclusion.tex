\section{Conclusion}
Before asking whether a trained forecaster behaves like population Bayes, like a library posterior or like a context-only
estimator, one should ask whether those references predict differently on the data at hand, and at what margin and
precision. Two classical quantities answer much of this before any map is computed: the relative entropy between the
population and context-only priors, a budget that the per-forecast gaps share out over the context, and the divergence to the
nearest library system, the long-context limit of the library gap. In scalar autoregressive systems with a known noise scale
the budget is small and spent at very short contexts, observation noise spreads it more thinly, and libraries of
\LibBigKOneA or more systems are not separable from continuous priors on held-out data in our map, while very small libraries
are. The larger gaps when the noise scale is unknown, or in the bivariate family, come with much larger budgets that are
mostly knowledge of the noise level; in the bivariate family libraries stay separable far beyond the scalar range because a
seven-parameter space is covered sparsely. A network trained to convergence on the population law will look like population
Bayes almost by construction; whether a network trained on a finite library memorises or generalises is the open question,
and the bivariate family is where its answer would be visible by score. We offer the references, the maps, the two bounds
and the pre-registration machinery as a precondition for behavioural claims about in-context learners of dynamical systems.
