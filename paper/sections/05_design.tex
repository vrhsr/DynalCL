\section{Separability-first design}\label{sec:design}
\paragraph{Margin and gap.} We use a margin $\delta=\DeltaNats$ nats per forecast, fixed before any result in the first
study and kept since. For a pair of references $P$ and $Q$ on a set of evaluation systems, the gap is the expected
log-score difference $\mathbb E[\log p(y\mid\text{context})-\log q(y\mid\text{context})]$ over contexts and targets from
those systems. When $P$ is the predictive of the process that generated the systems (population Bayes on held-out systems,
library Bayes on in-library systems), the gap equals an expected Kullback--Leibler divergence and is non-negative. On
designed sets (probe grids, out-of-support systems) neither reference is the truth, the gap can have either sign, and it
measures a behavioural difference rather than a divergence; we therefore report designed sets separately and do not use
them for the headline statements below.

\paragraph{Rules.} The reference-only maps (1A, F1, F3, F2) mark a cell \textsc{separable} if estimate $-\,3\,$SE $\ge\delta$, \textsc{not separable} if
estimate $+\,3\,$SE $<\delta$, and \textsc{borderline} otherwise (the released result files call the first two
\textsc{identifiable} and \textsc{unidentifiable}); the F1 map applies this to the absolute gap (two-sided), which only matters
on designed sets. For libraries each map draws several independent libraries per size $K$ (fewer in F3 and F2 than in the 1A
and F1 maps), applies the cell rule per draw, and marks a cell \textsc{separable} or \textsc{not separable} only if at least
\DrawPct per cent of draws agree, otherwise \textsc{partial}. Because a trained model sees one library, a \textsc{partial}
cell means that some libraries are separable from the continuous prior and others are not; we report per-draw agreement
rather than hide it. The maps use common random numbers, so their cells are dependent and patterns across cells are
descriptive. The rule is applied per cell without a global correction: with a correct standard error, the chance that a cell
is marked on the wrong side of $\delta$ is at most $\Phi(-3)$ whatever the dependence between cells, so the expected number of
wrongly marked held-out population cells is below one in every map. Each map's specification was committed before its seed
was first used. The pre-registered studies that preceded the maps used other rules with the same margin
(Appendix~\ref{app:history}).

\paragraph{What ``separable'' does and does not mean.} Here it is a property of two reference predictors at a precision:
their expected gap exceeds $\delta$ by more than three standard errors. (We avoid ``identifiable'': in its statistical sense
every pair of references here is identifiable, since their gaps are positive in every cell, however small.) Separability is
needed in our designs for a per-cell behavioural classification at margin $\delta$, but it is not sufficient: a trained
model is a random object whose seed variance enters any classification. For F1, the bootstrap-calibrated operating
characteristics of our pre-registered classification rule imply that, with \MinModels models per arm, a population-like
versus context-only classification has power of at least \LookupPower only if the between-seed standard deviation of a model's
regret is at most \LookupQoneThirtyTwo nats (\LookupQoneSixtyFour at twice as many models). Evidence pooled over cells or over
a whole sequence could separate references whose per-cell gaps are below $\delta$; Section~\ref{sec:theory} bounds how much
such pooling can gain.
