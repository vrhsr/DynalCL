\section{A pre-registered neural pilot that could not be resolved}\label{sec:pilot}
We pre-registered a neural study in the known-scale family (questions: population-like versus context-only behaviour in the
separable cells; library size at which memorisation gives way to generalisation), froze its code, configuration,
decision rules and seed table, and ran a pilot with dedicated seeds. The frozen rules chose the training budget as the
smallest grid point at which every control passed and the validation log loss at the shortest contexts, for every pilot
model, stopped improving by more than \ConvTol nats, then capped the number of models per arm by a compute budget, with a
minimum of \MinModels models per arm fixed before the pilot from the bootstrap-calibrated operating characteristics. The
budget was raised from \CapBudgetHoursOriginal to \CapBudgetHours GPU-hours by a pre-pilot amendment, made after seeing the
timing benchmark and before any pilot data, and frozen with the rest.

The pilot chose \PilotB training steps: at the first grid point a control failed, and at the second the largest improvement
over the pilot models was still \PilotImprovementTwentyFive nats, exceeding the tolerance by less than a tenth of it, with no
uncertainty attached to that comparison by the rule; at the chosen point it was \PilotImprovementFifty. At that budget the cap
allowed \PilotCap models per arm, below the minimum, so the pre-registered outcome is \textsc{not resolvable (compute)}. This
is the result of the pre-registered study. No effect-bearing evaluation of the pilot models was run. A further, post-pilot
budget increase was prepared, labelled as such, and not adopted. A pilot that ends by its own rules is evidence about what
the design can resolve. Section~\ref{sec:theory} also changes what a later neural study should ask: a network trained to
convergence on the population law approximates population Bayes, because that predictor minimises the expected log loss, so
the question whether it is population-like has a predictable answer; whether a network trained on a finite library
reproduces the library posterior or generalises is not fixed in this way, and the nearest-system limit says in which family
the answer is visible by score.
