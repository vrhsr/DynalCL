\section{Limitations}\label{sec:limits}
\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{Priors and comparators.} Each map uses one population prior and one context-only comparator, and the gaps
depend on both. Table~\ref{tab:sensitivity} varies both in the scalar families with a known or unknown noise scale, where the
windows move as the budgets of Section~\ref{sec:theory} predict; we have not varied them in the noisy or bivariate families.
\item \textbf{Margin.} The window depends on $\delta$ (Table~\ref{tab:delta}); $\delta$ was fixed in advance as a small
log-score difference, not derived from the seed variance of trained models. A test that pools evidence over many sequences
or items can detect gaps below $\delta$; the budget bounds the per-sequence effect, not the evidence such pooling
accumulates, so ``not separable'' here means not separable per cell at this margin and precision.
\item \textbf{Power and grids.} The F3 map is underpowered at moderate noise; with ten times its sample size the window there
widens (Table~\ref{tab:sensitivity}). The grids of context lengths are coarse and differ by map; the ends of the F1 and F2
windows were located by extensions to denser and longer grids (Section~\ref{sec:results}), and the F3 window at the smallest
margin still reaches its last grid point.
\item \textbf{Per-cell rules and dependence.} The maps use per-cell rules without a global correction and common random
numbers; the expected number of wrongly marked held-out population cells is below one per map, and patterns across cells are
descriptive.
\item \textbf{Designed sets.} Where library and continuous references separate on probe or out-of-support sets, the gap is
not a divergence and partly reflects how well the library covers the edge of the stable region.
\item \textbf{The long-context limits.} The nearest-system limit assumes posterior concentration and the stationary law of the
regressor; the extensions to long contexts agree with it in the mean, but it is an average over libraries and does not make
every library separable. The $K^{-2/p}$ scaling in the number of system parameters is an asymptotic heuristic, not a
theorem, and does not match the decay at the library sizes we evaluated. The split of the gap into noise-level and dynamics
parts depends on the intermediate references and on their order; in F2 the two orders disagree on which part is larger in
most cells (every $r$ at $n=10$, and $r=4$ at the next grid point), so we report both and draw no general conclusion about
which dominates.
\item \textbf{Horizons.} The maps score one-step forecasts, except the F3 map, which also scores five-step forecasts. The
budget bounds the summed one-step gaps directly; an $h$-step gap is at most the sum of $h$ consecutive one-step gaps, so the
summed $h$-step gaps are bounded only by $h$ times the budget. Horizons beyond five steps, and five steps outside the noisy
family, are not mapped.
\item \textbf{Simple families.} All results concern linear autoregressive processes with Gaussian noise, scalar or
bivariate; nothing is claimed for nonlinear, chaotic or high-dimensional systems. Each confirmatory study ran on one platform
with two replicates.
\item \textbf{Monte Carlo limits in F2.} The bivariate context-only reference is not computable to our criterion at the
shortest contexts, so the F2 population window is not mapped there. Because the cell-level Monte Carlo criterion tightens as
the number of evaluation items falls, several in-library F2 cells with fewer items are also \textsc{mc-limited}; the sample
sizes were fixed before this was seen.
\item \textbf{No neural result.} The one neural study ended not resolvable; this paper makes no claim about trained models.
\end{itemize}
