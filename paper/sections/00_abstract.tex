\begin{abstract}
Pretrained sequence models are often described as behaving ``like Bayes with prior $X$'', or as memorising their
training tasks rather than generalising. Classifying a model in this way, cell by cell, requires that the candidate reference
predictors themselves make different predictions there. For in-context forecasting of autoregressive systems (three scalar
families and a bivariate one) we build and validate three kinds of reference predictor (Bayes under the population prior, the
posterior over a finite library of training systems, and a context-only Bayes predictor that knows the noise model and
generic stability but nothing about the population) and map where they are separable at a margin of $\delta=\DeltaNats$~nats
per forecast. Two classical quantities organise the maps. By the chain rule of relative entropy, the per-forecast gaps between
population and context-only Bayes, summed over context lengths, cannot exceed the relative entropy between their priors, a
fixed budget that the data spend; and on held-out systems the gap between a library posterior and population Bayes tends, at
long contexts, to the divergence from the true system to the nearest library system. With a known noise scale the budget is
\KLoneA~nats and the population gap is separable only at very short contexts (\OneAGapMin--\OneAGapMax~nats per forecast
there); under additive observation noise the budget is unchanged but spread over more forecasts, and no cell is separable at
the two larger noise levels. When the noise scale is unknown the gaps are larger (up to \FoneGapMax~nats) but mostly
knowledge of the noise level: given the population's noise prior, the context-only reference is separable from population
Bayes at short contexts at most, as with a known scale. In the bivariate family the gaps are larger still (up to
\FtwoGapMax~nats) and, at the context lengths we can map, mostly knowledge of the dynamics. Varying the comparator and the
population prior moves the windows as the budgets predict. Small libraries are separable from continuous-prior Bayes on
held-out systems in many (cell, library) pairs; with a known noise scale, libraries of \LibBigKOneA or more systems are
separable nowhere in our map, while bivariate libraries of up to \KThousand systems become separable as the context grows,
as the nearest-system divergence predicts. We also report a pre-registered neural pilot that ended, under its own frozen rules, as \emph{not
resolvable} for compute reasons, and document the pre-registration and disclosure trail as part of the method.
\end{abstract}
