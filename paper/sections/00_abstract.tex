\begin{abstract}
Classifying a pretrained sequence model as behaving ``like Bayes with prior $X$'', or as memorising its training tasks,
requires that the candidate reference predictors themselves predict differently on the evaluation data. For in-context
forecasting of autoregressive systems (three scalar families and a bivariate one) we build and validate three references
(Bayes under the population prior, the posterior over a finite library of training systems, and a context-only Bayes
predictor that knows the noise model and stability but not the population) and map where they are separable at a margin of
$\delta=\DeltaNats$~nats per forecast. Two classical quantities organise the maps: the per-forecast gaps between population
and context-only Bayes, summed over context lengths, cannot exceed the relative entropy between their priors; and on held-out
systems the library gap tends at long contexts to the divergence to the nearest library system. With a known noise scale the
budget is \KLoneA~nats and the population gap is separable only at very short contexts; observation noise spreads the same
budget more thinly. With an unknown noise scale the gaps are larger (up to \FoneGapMax~nats) but mostly knowledge of the noise
level; in the bivariate family (up to \FtwoGapMax~nats) the split between noise level and dynamics depends on the order in
which they are given to the comparator. Small libraries are separable from continuous-prior Bayes; with a known noise scale
libraries of \LibBigKOneA or more systems are separable nowhere in our map, while bivariate libraries of up to \KThousand
systems become separable at long contexts, as the nearest-system divergence predicts. We also report pre-registered studies,
including a neural pilot that ended, under its own frozen rules, as \emph{not resolvable} for compute reasons.
\end{abstract}
