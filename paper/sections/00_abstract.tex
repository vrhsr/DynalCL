\begin{abstract}
Pretrained sequence models are often described as behaving ``like Bayes with prior $X$'', or as memorising their
training tasks rather than generalising. Such statements are only testable where the candidate reference predictors
themselves make different predictions. For in-context forecasting of scalar autoregressive systems we build and validate
three kinds of reference predictor (Bayes under the population prior, the posterior over a finite library of training
systems, and a context-only Bayes predictor that knows the noise model and generic stability but nothing about the
population), and we map, at a common margin of $\delta=\DeltaNats$~nats per forecast, where they can be told apart.
With a known noise scale, population knowledge is worth \OneAGapMin--\OneAGapMax~nats and is separable only at very short
contexts; under additive observation noise the largest gap falls to \FthreeGapQthree~nats at the highest noise level and
no cell is separable at the two larger noise levels, while at moderate noise our map is underpowered. When the noise scale
is unknown the window is much wider (gaps up to \FoneGapMax~nats, separable up to $n=\FoneMaxN$). Libraries of one to four
systems are distinguishable from continuous-prior Bayes on held-out systems in many cells; with a known noise scale,
libraries of sixteen or more systems never are in our map, while with an unknown noise scale only very large libraries are
uniformly indistinguishable. The map conclusions depend on $\delta$, on the comparator and on the single population prior,
and we report how. We also report a pre-registered neural pilot that ended, under its own frozen rules, as \emph{not
resolvable} for compute reasons, and document the pre-registration and disclosure trail as part of the method.
\end{abstract}
