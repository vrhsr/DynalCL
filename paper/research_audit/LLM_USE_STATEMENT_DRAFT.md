# LLM use: TMLR policy and a draft statement (for the authors' approval; not yet in the manuscript)

## 1. TMLR's policy, quoted verbatim

Source: https://jmlr.org/tmlr/editorial-policies.html , section "LLM use policy for authors" (fetched 2026-11-01; raw HTML
saved in the session scratchpad `tmlr/editorial.html`):

> LLMs may be used as general-purpose assistive tools. Whichever tools are used, authors are fully responsible for content on
> which they are listed as (co-) authors. This includes, but is not limited to, content generated by LLMs that could be
> construed as plagiarism or scientific misconduct (e.g., fabrication of facts). Low-quality contributions (be they submissions
> or reviews) that appear to be largely LLM-generated will be closely examined for evidence of the issues mentioned previously,
> such as scientific misconduct. LLMs are not eligible for authorship. We will periodically revise this policy as new
> information about the use of LLMs in the scientific process becomes available.

Same page, "Criteria for Authorship": authors "take full responsibility for all content in the final published work", and
"Authors may choose to include a statement indicating author contributions."

Related requirements (author guide, https://jmlr.org/tmlr/author-guide.html, same date): "TMLR uses a double blind review process
and submissions must be anonymized"; supplementary material "must be anonymized".

## 2. What this means here (my reading)

* The policy does not require a disclosure statement, but it puts full responsibility on the authors and says largely
  LLM-generated work will be "closely examined". This project's code, analyses, derivations, literature search and text were
  produced in Claude Code sessions under the authors' direction (repository: CLAUDE.md, disclosure logs). A voluntary, specific
  statement is the honest course and pre-empts the "closely examined" concern; the disclosure-log trail is the evidence.
* The statement must not overstate the authors' checking: every bracketed item below is something the authors must actually do
  (or delete) before submission.
* For double-blind review, the statement must not name the authors, the repository or the box.

## 3. Draft statement (to go in an unnumbered "Use of large language models" section before the references)

> **Use of large language models.** This project was carried out with extensive use of an LLM-based coding agent (Claude,
> Anthropic, used through Claude Code). Under the authors' direction the agent wrote and tested the reference implementations,
> validation and mutation tests, the map and analysis code, the pre-registration and specification drafts, the build pipeline
> that re-extracts every number in this paper from pinned result files, literature searches (each citation checked against its
> primary record), and drafts of the text. The authors set the research questions, approved every pre-registration, freeze,
> amendment and analysis decision at recorded checkpoints, ran or authorised all compute, and [read and checked every claim,
> derivation and citation in the final text]. The internal reviews used during the work, including the hostile reviews, were
> performed by separate LLM agent instances and are not peer reviews. Every decision, failure and correction is recorded in
> append-only disclosure logs released with the code [anonymised for review]. The authors take full responsibility for the
> content.
