## Thesis (one paragraph)
Claims that a pretrained sequence model "behaves like Bayes with prior X" (or "memorises its training tasks") are only testable
where the candidate reference predictors themselves differ by more than a meaningful margin. For AR-family dynamical systems we
build and validate the references (continuous-prior Bayes, finite-library posterior, context-only Bayes), map where they are
separable under a pre-registered rule, and show that the population advantage is small and confined to short contexts in
the known-noise families (S0, S05, S06, S1A-MAP-Q1), larger and longer-lived when the noise scale is unknown (F1-MAP-Q1), and
that library vs continuous is rarely identifiable on natural evaluation sets (S0-ELIB). A pre-registered neural pilot then
ended NOT RESOLVABLE (compute) (S1A-PILOT), reported exactly as it happened.
