\section{Neural studies: design}\label{sec:neural-design}
\paragraph{Family and questions.} The first neural study is a seed-variance probe: it measures how much a trained model's
distance to each reference varies between training seeds, and every power statement of the later studies rests on that
number (Section~\ref{sec:design}). The memorisation study follows it. Q2: do transformers pretrained on a library of
systems behave like the library posterior or like a generalising reference on held-out systems, and from which library
size on? Its family is chosen by a rule fixed before the probe is run: the bivariate family (F2), where large libraries
remain separable at long contexts (Section~\ref{sec:results}), if the upper bound of the probe's between-seed spread is
within the tolerance of the pre-registered lookup and the compute budget holds the required models, and otherwise the
unknown-noise family (F1), where the transition is classifiable only for small libraries.
